## The mint is not the product

Real-world asset tokenization promised liquidity, fractional ownership, and global access. Teams built minting rails, metadata standards, and marketplace listings. Investors asked for "on-chain real estate."

Then nothing moved at institutional scale.

The asset was tokenized. The **verification stack was not reusable**. Every lender, marketplace, booking platform, and compliance desk rebuilt the same KYC, title review, insurance check, and accreditation flow from scratch.

Tokenization created a representation. It did not create **trust that travels**.

## What institutions actually need

Institutional participants do not wake up wanting a token. They want:

- Clear title and lien status they can rely on
- Identity and eligibility outcomes — not document folders
- Audit trails that survive personnel changes
- Proof that works across counterparties without re-collection

Minting solves none of that by itself. It adds a layer on top of assets that still require **repeated human verification** at every touchpoint.

## The hidden tax

Every repeated upload creates:

- **Time cost** — days to weeks per counterparty
- **Drop-off** — users abandon flows after the third ID request
- **Trust erosion** — "verified" on one platform means nothing on the next
- **Operational drag** — support teams chase PDFs instead of serving customers

This is the hidden tax on RWA adoption. Tokenization without reusable verification is a brochure, not infrastructure.
